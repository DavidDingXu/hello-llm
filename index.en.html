Part I

Make a numerical network learn

Start with one output. See how errors change parameters.

01—06
  1. 01Neurons: What Does a Model Calculate Before It Writes the Next Character?Explain the roles of inputs, parameters, and activation.
  2. 02Vectors and Matrices: Do Two Outputs Require Two Copies of the Code?Explain input, parameter, and output shapes axis by axis.
  3. 03Nonlinearity: Why Can Multiply-and-Add Not Express Four Simple Rules?Explain the difference between stacking layers and adding nonlinearity.
  4. 04Gradients: Why Can an Update in the Right Direction Make Things Worse?Distinguish the local direction given by a gradient from the actual update step size.
  5. 05Backpropagation: Must We Test All 9 Parameters One by One?Calculate one backward path by hand and explain how branch contributions combine before parameters are updated together.
  6. 06The Training Loop: What Happens to Four Predictions After 500 Updates?Write one training iteration, explain gradient clearing, and identify incorrect comparisons caused by broadcasting.

Stage goal: Complete your first training loop

Part II

Bring text into the network

Turn text into inputs, probabilities, and training examples.

07—09
  1. 07Embedding: How Does Text Become a Network Input?Distinguish character IDs, embedding vectors, and the three tensor axes.
  2. 08Softmax: How Do Scores Become Next-Character Probabilities?Explain candidate scores, relative competition, and normalization.
  3. 09Training Targets: How Does a Sentence Become a Learnable Task?Construct shifted targets and explain average loss over valid positions.

Stage goal: Train your first character prediction model

Part III

Read context and assemble the model

Add context and order, then connect the components.

10—16
  1. 10Attention: How Does Predicting the Next Character Read Earlier Text?Calculate one attention row and distinguish reading proportions from the content being read.
  2. 11Causal Masks: Why Is Parallel Training Not Peeking at the Answer?Explain causal visibility and the correct placement of the mask.
  3. 12Multi-Head Attention: Where Does Each Head Read?Explain head splitting, independent attention weights, and merging, and identify incorrect axes even when shapes match.
  4. 13RoPE: How Does the Model Know the Order?Connect the two-dimensional rotation formula to relative positions and Q/K dot products.
  5. 14SwiGLU: What Happens After Reading the Prefix?Explain input-dependent gating and positionwise processing.
  6. 15Residual Connections and RMSNorm: Why Keep a Direct Path?Assemble head splitting, rotation, and causal aggregation into an attention component, then add residual connections and a Pre-Norm model block.
  7. 16The Complete Model: Follow the Character “欢” Through Every StepAssemble the complete model and distinguish representations, parameters, and vocabulary scores.

Stage goal: Trace a forward pass through the complete model

Part IV

Pretrain, generate, and fine-tune

Teach the same model to continue text, then answer questions.

17—20
  1. 17Pretraining: Why Can New Questions Get Worse as Training Questions Improve?Complete pretraining and use differences in the data to explain changes in evaluation results.
  2. 18Generation: How Many Choices Does One Sentence Take?Write a generation loop and explain random selection and stopping conditions.
  3. 19Supervised Fine-Tuning: Why Can It Continue Text but Still Fail to Answer?Build answer supervision and diagnose fine-tuning tradeoffs with fixed comparisons across three methods and three seeds.
  4. 20The Full Workflow: Connect Pretraining, Fine-Tuning, and GenerationIndependently complete a transfer experiment covering data, vocabulary, model construction, training, and reloading, and explain both successes and failures.

Stage goal: Reproduce training and test your understanding on new data
